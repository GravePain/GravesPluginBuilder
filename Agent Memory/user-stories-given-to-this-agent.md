## G-1 — "the game has gold and you craft at a station"

**Given:** 2026-09-05, immediately after a design conversation about whether currency is an item.
**Marked as a story by Graves**, not inferred: *"thats my user story"*.

### What it settles

Two things, and both had been open questions this agent was answering by argument rather than by
instruction.

**There is gold.** Currency is real in this game, not a hypothetical. It had just been built as an
ordinary item living in a purse, on the argument that the slot objection is a property of the
container rather than of the coin. This story confirms the feature is wanted; it does not confirm
the model, which remains this agent's call and can be overturned.

**Crafting happens at a station.** This had been deliberately left out an hour earlier, with the
reasoning recorded that "standing at a forge needs a world" and was a decision a particular game
makes. **This is that game making it.** The right response is to build it, not to note that the
prediction was correct.

### What it does NOT settle, and must not be assumed

- Whether gold is spent on anything other than crafting.
- Whether a station is a fixed prop, a placeable, a portable kit, or a person.
- Whether different stations do different things, or one station does everything.
- Whether being near a station is enough, or it must be used deliberately.

Each is a real fork and none is in the sentence. They are being decided by this agent and recorded
as decisions, so a wrong one is visible rather than absorbed.

### How it was built

A recipe carries requirement modules, checked before materials are even counted, because "you must
be at a forge" is more useful to be told than "you need three iron" when both are true and only one
is fixable where you stand.

The station half lives in its own assembly. A game that crafts anywhere installs crafting and never
inherits the concept of a place.
